The Long View

The case for optimism

This edition makes the case for optimism. Stefan Sagmeister’s ‘Finally, Something Good’ at La Gaîté Lyrique answers short-term negative news and algorithm-driven outrage with centuries of UN and World Bank data, set into 18th- and 19th-century oil paintings, to show that progress becomes undeniable when viewed across extended timeframes. That long view runs through the week: Margaret Calvert and Ian Cartlidge rebrand the Royal Designers for Industry at 90, Nebbia rethinks monumentality for a commission meant to last 1,000 years, and Peugeot and Amstel mine their heritage without slipping into retro. Against that, AI keeps offering the shortcut – Just Eat has apologised for menus filled with grotesque text-to-image dishes, a survey of 521 UX practitioners finds AI makes tasks faster without making days shorter, and AI companies keep hiring human designers for their own identities. Dutch Design Week, the Dezeen Awards public votes and the start of Oxford Street’s pedestrianisation round out a busy three days.

Editor’s note

Lead story KU

Stefan Sagmeister’s artworks turn global data into good news at La Gaîté Lyrique

Designboom · 26 Oct 2026 · images courtesy of Stefan Sagmeister

Thirteen years after ‘The Happy Show’, Stefan Sagmeister returns to Paris’ La Gaîté Lyrique with his solo exhibition, ‘Finally, Something Good’, on view through April 18, 2027.

Sagmeister’s show confronts the relentless stream of short-term negative news head-on. Drawing on centuries of data from the United Nations, the World Bank and global archives, it shows conditions improving across health, democracy and equality – staged in chapters across every floor of the venue, down to the restrooms, ‘where no visitor escapes data-driven optimism’. Guided by the belief that design should either inform or delight, he pairs beauty with evidence to make the statistics land.

“There’s something almost perverse about being an optimist right now – the culture has decided that pessimism is the serious position.”
“Beauty and evidence, rarely combined, are more devastatingly strong together than either is alone.”

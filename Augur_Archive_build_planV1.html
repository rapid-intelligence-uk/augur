How augur Remembers
The memory layer of the augur ecosystem · Rapid Intelligence, Cision

"History doesn't repeat itself, but it often rhymes." — attributed to Mark Twain

The augur Method makes one hard demand of the engine: it must remember patterns rather than invent them. The Archive is where that memory lives. This plan describes what to build, in what order — and why starting with a year of historic briefings is the move that turns the Archive from a filing cabinet into a proof engine.

The Archive is three stores, not one

A pile of documents gives you search. Augury needs structure. The Archive is built in three increasing layers — and the classic mistake is conflating them, or asking the document store to do the structured store's job.

1
The Corpus
The documents themselves · the disciplina
Every published Signscape, stored verbatim, immutable and timestamped — the augur's book of precedent. Lowest effort, immediate value, and the thing you can start banking today with zero intelligence applied.
Answers: "What did we say, and when?"
2
The Signal Ledger
The structured extraction · where it becomes augury
Every briefing shredded into its component signals and events as structured rows — date, lane, institution, signal type, event, source, client. This is the layer agents reason over, because pattern recognition needs fields to compare, not prose to re-read.
Answers: "Find every motor-finance regulatory signal in the last 18 months."
3
The Prediction Ledger
The scoring loop · the Method's non-negotiable
Every prediction logged before the event — with lead-time window and confidence — then later marked hit / miss / partial. This is what stops augur sliding into hindsight bias. Without it you have a catalogue; with it you have an auditable forecasting record — and the single most powerful slide you can show a client.
Answers: "What is our verified hit rate?"
Start with history — the power move
Why historic-first beats daily accrual

Building the Archive forward, one day at a time, means waiting months for the first rhyme. Mining a year of past briefings gives you the rhymes now — before the meeting cycle. But mining the past is also where you can most easily fool yourself, because finding patterns in data you've already seen is trivial. The discipline that converts it from astrology into evidence is the hold-out back-test:

Derive patterns from months 1–9. Then ask: would they have correctly called what actually happened in months 10–12? That is the Prediction Ledger run retroactively — and if it works, you walk in with "augur found patterns in our own back-catalogue and they predicted the next quarter", not "augur could find patterns."
Composing the corpus: ~20 clients, clustered

Twenty scattered clients gives twenty thin silos. Twenty clustered clients — roughly 4–5 sectors × 4–5 clients — unlocks the cross-client rhymes augur actually trades on.

Within a sector

Do these five banks share a regulatory and calendar rhythm? Dense, high-confidence patterns.

Across sectors

Does a signal in one rhyme with a different event elsewhere? Rarer, more valuable cross-pollination.

You already built the hard half

augur-data.json proved the structured shape. The pattern-library Lists prove the storage pattern. The Signscape template, with its labelled lanes, guarantees parseable output — the template discipline is precisely what makes auto-extraction tractable. This is connecting existing pieces, not inventing.

Storage: SharePoint List + Library (for now)

Resist reaching for a "real" database in v1. SharePoint is already in the tenant (zero procurement), Copilot Studio reads it natively (no connector engineering), and it's governed and permissioned for free. The honest ceiling — Lists slow past ~100k rows and lack semantic vector search — is years of daily briefings away. When you hit it, the clean structured rows migrate to Dataverse or a vector store trivially. Don't build the Ferrari before proving the route.

The decisions that actually matter
DecisionWhy it's load-bearing
The metadata schema & controlled vocabularyThe whole game. Lane/category and signal-type must use one shared taxonomy across Archive, Vantage and Signscape — so one Copilot output feeds all three. Garbage-in here caps everything downstream.
A stable ID schemeEach briefing has an ID; each signal references its parent briefing; each prediction references the signals it drew on. That referential spine makes the visible-reasoning principle queryable — trace any prediction back to its evidence.
Retro-ingest the back-cataloguePatterns need history to rhyme against. A one-off bulk parse seeds Layer 2 with instant depth — it's what makes the first patterns findable rather than waiting months.
Immutability vs. correctionPublished briefings shouldn't be edited — the record must be honest. Use SharePoint versioning for errata: append, don't overwrite.
Recommended build order
Pick the ~20 & locate the PDFs
Clustered by sector, biased to regulated/calendared clients with an unbroken year. Confirm where a clean year × 20 actually lives — this is the real bottleneck, not the tech.
Prove the parser on one client's year
Drop one client's PDFs in; build and tune the extractor before scaling. Nail the parse on a handful of files first.
Scale to 20 → build the Signal Ledger
Normalise every briefing into structured rows using the shared controlled vocabulary.
Run the mine + hold-out back-test
Seasonality, frequency, lead-lag — then validate against the held-out final quarter. Output: seed Pattern Library + the accuracy slide.
Wire Layer 1 live, then Layer 3
Auto-file new Signscapes on publish (Cannes monitor / next LBG cycle as the forcing function); stand up the Prediction Ledger once PathFinder produces scored predictions.
The strategic point

A year of past briefings, mined honestly and back-tested, is not a data-cleanup chore — it is Cision's unfair advantage made visible. No competitor has Rapid Intelligence's depth of historic, structured intelligence on these clients. The Archive is the moat; the back-test is the proof; the seed Pattern Library is the head start.

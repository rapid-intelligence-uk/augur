The augur Lexicon · A Living Nomenclature
The shared tongue of the practice
Every word augur uses carries a worldview. This is the nomenclature — each term with its meaning, its connotation, and the way it is actually spoken — because to learn the language is to inherit the discipline.
Read the signs · Forward Intelligence · By Cision

"History doesn't repeat itself, but it often rhymes."— attributed to Mark Twain

A practice is held together by its language. The words a team uses do not merely describe how it works — they shape how it thinks. When an analyst asks “has this been promoted to a pattern?” or says “we need to score that prediction,” they are, without noticing, becoming the kind of practitioner augur asks for: one who distinguishes a one-off from a rhyme, who holds foresight to account. This lexicon sets down that tongue — not as a glossary to file away, but as a culture to speak into being.

How to read this lexicon

Each entry gives the term, its type, a definition, and — crucially — how it is said in the daily work, since usage is where culture lives. Where a word is easily confused with another, the boundary is drawn. Terms are colour-coded by kind:

Part I
The Stance
The words that carry the worldview. Before the nouns and verbs, these establish how augur sees: that the world emits raw signs, and significance is something made through disciplined relationship — never passively received.
augur/ˈɔː.ɡə/ · always lowercaseThe practice · the product
Both the discipline and the platform. An augur reads signs as a craft — not a seer in a trance, but a practitioner applying reasoning to raw material. The lowercase wordmark is deliberate: augur is a way of working, not a shouting brand.
"Run it through augur." · "That's an augur question." · "What does augur say?"
Not a crystal ball, a forecast tool, or an oracle. augur does not know the future; it reads the present with discipline.
Read the signsThe tagline · the imperative
augur's governing instruction, not a slogan. It compresses the whole worldview: significance is read, not received. The world offers signs; meaning is the act of attending to them with craft.
"Read the signs." — the sign-off on a Signscape; the answer to "so what should we do?"
the rhymen. — the central idea
A recurring pattern with variation — the same meter, different words. History does not repeat (that would be fate); it rhymes (that is pattern). The rhyme is why the Archive can remember without inventing, and why a lead-time is a window, not a prophecy.
"Does this rhyme with anything?" · "That's a rhyme of the 2024 motor-finance cycle."
Not a repeat. A rhyme is approximate by nature — which is exactly why some predictions miss, and why we score them.
signn. — raw material
Something the world emits that might mean something. A sign is inert until noticed. The world is full of signs; the vast majority never become signals because no one attends to them.
"There's a sign here." — said when something in the noise feels worth capturing.
Not the same as a signal. A sign is potential; a signal is a sign that augur has caught and entered into the record.
deciphermentn. — the craft itself · logismos
The reasoning that turns a signal into meaning. The ancients called it logismos — the rational art that the seer, the healer and the carpenter all practised as craftsmen. In augur it is the visible reasoning chain: signal → pattern → prediction.
"Show the decipherment." — i.e. expose the reasoning, never just announce the call.
Not intuition or a hunch. If you can't show the chain, you haven't deciphered — you've guessed.
the templumn. — the field of attention
The marked-out portion of the world an augur chooses to watch. The Roman augur fixed a templum in the sky before reading it. In augur, an Edition is a templum — a deliberate boundary on what counts as a relevant sign.
"That's outside this templum." — said when a signal belongs to a different Edition's world.
Underpins why Editions are not filters but worlds: each marks out its own sky.
the arrow of timen. — the test
The single criterion that separates a signal from a coincidence: a signal is committed to before the event and scored; a coincidence is noticed after, and can never be wrong. A prediction can fail; a coincidence cannot — and only what can fail can earn trust.
"Does it pass the arrow of time?" · "Would we have called this in advance?"
Not about whether something feels meaningful. Retrospective meaning is free; forward commitment is the only thing that counts.
synchronicity with a scorecardphr. — the posture
augur's stance toward the meaningful coincidence. It permits the thrill of seeing connection — intuition, the felt rhyme, the uncanny convergence — and then makes that intuition prove itself forward. Wonder is welcome as a source; it is never accepted as a verdict.
"Lovely hunch — now give it a scorecard."
Not mysticism, and not its dismissal. augur is agnostic about where intuition comes from; it only governs what you may do with it.
the framen. — the instrument of meaning
The disciplined structure that makes the relationship between signs visible. A sign means nothing alone; significance appears only when a frame holds two signs in one view. The craft is building the frame that reveals a real relationship without inventing one that isn't there.
"What frame makes this connect?" · "That's the frame that made their relationship visible."
Not a bias to be eliminated. The frame is the instrument — the skill is holding it honestly, not pretending to have none.
the virtuous loopn. — the augur engine
The self-correcting cycle: hold a pattern → predict → score → update → retire or trust. Reality votes at every turn, so confidence is earned rather than assumed. A pattern may prime attention only in proportion to its scored hit-rate.
"Keep it in the virtuous loop — has it actually scored?"
The opposite of the vicious loop (see Words of Caution). The whole difference is whether the loop contains an error signal.
Part II
The Objects
The nouns — the things augur reasons about. Meaning lives not in any single object but in the relationships between them: a signal is nothing until it is matched, sourced, owned.
Signaln.
A sign that has been noticed, captured and entered into the ledger — tagged with date, lane, institution and type. The atom of augur. Everything else is built from signals.
"Log it as a signal." · "How many signals this week?"
A signal is not yet meaning. It becomes meaningful only through its relationships — matched to a pattern, sourcing a prediction.
Eventn.
Something that happens (or is scheduled to) in the world — a results day, a regulatory letter, an appointment. Signals point toward events; predictions are about them.
"What's the event behind this signal?"
Patternn.
A rhyme made explicit — a recurring relationship between signals and events that augur has named and now watches for. The contents of the Archive's memory. Carries a lineage, a confidence, and a hit-rate.
"That's pattern P-014." · "Is there a pattern for this?"
A pattern is not a law. It is a tendency with a track record — held provisionally, deprecated when it stops rhyming.
Predictionn.
A falsifiable call about a future event, made before it happens, carrying a lead-time window and a confidence. The unit augur is ultimately judged on.
"What's the prediction, and when does it resolve?"
A prediction with no resolution date is not a prediction — it's an opinion. If it can't be scored, it doesn't count.
Signscapen. — the deliverable
The field of signs laid out to be read — the briefing augur delivers, in any clothing (Word, Teams card, SharePoint post are all a Signscape). What the recipient actually receives.
"Send the Signscape." · "It's in this week's Signscape."
Not a report. A report describes; a Signscape resolves to decisions.
Editionn.
A templum — a bounded world of signs with its own vocabulary and spectrum (Finance, Design, LBG…). One engine, many Editions.
"Which Edition?" — the first question of any reading.
Editions are not filters over one objective pile. Each constitutes its own set of relevant signs.
Institution / Sourcen.
The origin of a signal — a regulator, a company, a publication. Anchors provenance: every signal can be traced to where it came from.
"Which institution issued it?"
Analystn. — the craftsman
The human practitioner who owns the reading. augur does not remove the analyst; it arms them. The analyst is the demioergos — the craftsman who applies judgement the machine cannot.
"Who's the analyst on this?" — every reading has an owner.
The analyst is not a checkbox on automation. Editing the Signscape is the craft.
Part III
The Actions
The verbs — the only legal ways to change augur’s state. Each maps to a formal Action Type, but in the culture it is spoken plainly. The verbs are where the discipline becomes a habit.
take a readingv. — PublishSignscape
To produce and publish a Signscape. The defining act of augur — naming it as "taking a reading" rather than "running a report" is the whole culture in three words.
"Have we taken today's reading?" · "Who's on the reading this morning?"
surface / extractv. — ExtractSignal
To pull a signal out of a briefing or feed and enter it into the ledger. The act that turns a sign into a signal.
"Surface that as a signal."
matchv. — MatchToPattern
To recognise that a signal belongs to a known pattern — the moment of rhyme-detection.
"That matches P-022."
promotev. — PromoteToPattern
To elevate a recurring observation from experimental to a named, active Pattern. A deliberate, witnessed act — augur declaring "we now believe this rhymes."
"Promote it — we've seen it three cycles running."
Not done lightly. Premature promotion is how noise gets mistaken for signal.
call / predictv. — PredictEvent
To make a falsifiable prediction about an event, with a lead-time and confidence. To put augur's neck out.
"What are we calling, and by when?"
scorev. — ScorePrediction
To mark a resolved prediction hit, miss or partial. The act that keeps augur honest — the confessional of the practice. You always score the misses.
"Time to score last quarter's calls."
Skipping the misses is not an option. A practice that only counts its hits is astrology.
flag a clashv. — FlagClash
To raise that a client action collides with an external event, and recommend a decision (reschedule, brief, hold). augur's first true Action — a verb, not an observation.
"Flag a clash — their results land the same day as the FCA letter."
The model for every lane: don't just observe — resolve to a recommended decision.
Part IV
The Apparatus
The named components of the augur universe. Spatially: you stand at Vantage and look out across the Signscape toward the horizon.
Coren. — the engine
The layer that gathers raw signs from the feeds (Brandwatch, CisionOne, PR Newswire). The mechanism beneath everything.
"Core picked it up overnight."
Archiven. — the memory
augur's pattern memory — the corpus of past readings and the ledgers of signals, patterns and predictions. The disciplina: the book of precedent that lets augur remember rather than invent.
"Check the Archive — has this rhymed before?"
PathFindern. — the interpreter
The reasoning layer that turns signals into predictions with lead-times — the logismos made into software.
"PathFinder's calling a rebrand within 90 days."
Almanacn. — the calendar
The calendar layer — the recurring, dateable signs (results seasons, regulatory cycles, festivals). The Roman register of which days carried weight.
"The Almanac flags this week."
Vantagen. — the observation surface
The dashboard — the auguraculum, the consecrated point you stand at to watch. Where signals refract through the prism and the horizon unspools.
"Pull it up on Vantage."
Signscapen. — the deliverable
(See The Objects.) Listed here too because it is both what you receive and where the apparatus terminates — the point at which augur faces outward.
"You stand at Vantage and look out across the Signscape."
Part V
The States
Every object moves through a lifecycle — a small state machine. Fluency means knowing not just what a thing is, but where it is in its life. To say a prediction is “open” or a pattern is “deprecated” is to say something precise.
Signal
detected→matched→predicted→resolved
Prediction
open→hit / miss / partial
Pattern
experimental→active→deprecated
Signscape
draft→published→archived
The one that matters most is Prediction: a call begins open and must end hit, miss or partial. Nothing stays open forever — that is the discipline that separates augur from prophecy.
Part VI
The Rituals
A culture is carried by practices, not documents. Three recurring acts embed the discipline in how the team actually works — and naming them is what turns a workflow into a craft.
The Readingritual — daily / weekly
The act of taking a reading and publishing the Signscape. The heartbeat of the practice.
"Morning Reading's done."
The Scoringritual — periodic
The recurring moment when open predictions are marked hit, miss or partial. augur's confessional — the ritual that keeps it honest and visibly different from astrology.
"Scoring's on Friday — get your calls in."
Pattern Promotionritual — as earned
The deliberate, witnessed act of moving something from experimental to active. A small ceremony of "we now believe this rhymes."
"We promoted two patterns this month."
Part VII
Words of Caution
A language for foresight must also name its failure modes — because the line between augury and superstition is thin, and you cannot avoid what you cannot name. These are the words augur uses to police itself.
hyperactive agency detectionn. — a failure mode
The hardwired human tendency to perceive a hidden agent where none exists — to see intention in coincidence. augur's besetting risk, because a pattern-finder is, mechanically, an agency-detection machine.
"Careful — that might be hyperactive agency detection." — said when a 'pattern' may just be noise.
retrofitting necessityn. — a failure mode
Telling the story of an event so that it seems it had to happen — removing the randomness that was really there. Prophecy's oldest trick, and the enemy of honest scoring.
"That's retrofitting — we didn't call it, we're just explaining it after the fact."
the museumn. — a failure mode
What augur becomes if it produces beautiful artefacts that change no decisions. A pattern library nobody acts on; a dashboard nobody acts from. An ontology without actions is a museum.
"Is this driving a decision, or are we building a museum?"
the Leuctra trapn. — a failure mode · hindsight
Naming, after the event, all the omens that 'should have been seen.' Hindsight masquerading as foresight. The reason augur logs predictions before events — so the record can't be rewritten.
"Avoid the Leuctra trap — would we actually have called this in advance?"
the vicious loopn. — a failure mode · the delusion engine
The cycle that manufactures false certainty: hold a belief → prime attention → notice confirmations → believe harder → notice harder — with no error signal. Confidence climbs while accuracy stands still. A loop without falsification is a delusion generator.
"Watch the vicious loop — is our confidence tracking our accuracy, or just rising?"
The tell: rising certainty against flat results — a coincidence wearing a signal's clothes.
the phantom rhymen. — a failure mode · false recurrence
A pattern that appears to recur but does not — a false repeat planted in the ledger by sloppy extraction. Most often born of treating a mention of a past event as a fresh occurrence, inflating a pattern's precedent count with echoes of a single fact.
"Check that's not a phantom rhyme before it scores a pattern."
mention is not eventrule — extraction discipline
A briefing summarises articles, so an entity or date inside it is often a contextual reference, not a fresh happening. The extractor must separate the publication event (when a thing actually happened) from the mention event (when an article referenced it). Only the publication event may score a pattern.
"That report's a month old — mention, not event."
Not pedantry. Get this wrong and the ledger fills with phantom rhymes — the failure made faster by clean input.
To speak augur is to be

A craftsman who shows the decipherment. Someone who says “it rhymes” not “it repeats,” who scores the misses, who asks “which Edition?” and “what decision does this drive?” Someone for whom meaning is made, with discipline.

To speak augur is never to be

A fortune-teller who announces calls without reasoning. Someone who hears agents in noise, retrofits necessity onto chance, counts only the hits, and builds museums of artefacts that change no decisions.

The whole tongue, in one breath
Fluency is the marker of belonging

To learn augur’s work is to learn its language. The vocabulary is not decoration over the process — it is the process. Each noun carries an object, each verb a discipline, each state a moment of accountability. Speak it precisely and you cannot help but practise it honestly: the words will not let you announce a prophecy, skip a miss, or mistake a chart for a decision.

The analyst took a reading, surfaced a signal, matched it to a pattern, called the event with a lead-time — and next quarter, scored the prediction honestly.

Every word in that sentence is in this lexicon. That is the test: when the work can be said as a grammatical sentence in augur’s own words, the culture is real.

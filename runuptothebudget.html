01The Read

Five things to carry into daily executive briefings this fortnight. Ordered by consequence for banking clients, not by date.

Act

The Budget on 28 October settles, or does not settle, the bank-tax question

The date is fixed. The content is not. The Chancellor met the chiefs of Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, Nationwide and NatWest on 6 October and is reported to have given no guidance. On 9 October UK Finance, the CBI, TheCityUK, AFME and the City Corporation wrote to him against any rise, while the TUC published costed options for a higher surcharge. No bank-specific measure has been announced.

Brief as: speculation, attributed, with the source named each time. Do not describe a windfall tax or surcharge rise as policy. See Budget watch.

Act

Six banks report between 22 and 30 October, straddling the Budget

Barclays (22 Oct), HSBC (27 Oct), Standard Chartered, Santander Group and Santander UK (all 28 Oct, Budget day), Lloyds (29 Oct) and NatWest (30 Oct, 07:00). Results from 28 October onward will be read through whatever the Budget announces. Santander UK's is the first full quarter including TSB.

Brief as: each result with its guidance anchor and motor finance line. Prepare two framings for 29 and 30 October, with and without a bank measure. See Results week.

Watch

The 20–21 October data run feeds a live MPC decision on 5 November

Labour market (20 Oct), then September CPI, public finances and house prices (21 Oct). On 17 September the MPC held at 3.75% by 6–3, with three members voting for 4% (secondary sources name Greene, Mann and Pill). Press commentary points to a possible rise on 5 November; that is commentary, not a Bank statement. The Commons is in recess on decision day, so there is no immediate parliamentary reaction.

Brief as: data facts first, rate expectations attributed to named forecasters. Pre-build both a hold and a hike version.

Watch

Motor finance: the Tribunal timetable ruling is still to come

The Upper Tribunal case-management hearing took place on 5–6 October. As at 10 October no ruling has been found on whether the main hearing is 14–18 December 2026 or 16–26 February 2027. The FCA scheme (PS26/3) stays partly suspended; the FCA says payments would come in 2027 at the earliest. Santander UK (28 Oct) and Lloyds (29 Oct) are the first provision updates.

Brief as: pending, with both hearing windows stated. Never imply a final cost. Check the FCA site and the Tribunal order each morning.

Note

Customer and workforce stories already running

HSBC UK wealth management job cuts (FT, 7 Oct; reported exits by end of October; HSBC has not given numbers). Halifax online and app services close on 1 November with customers moving to Lloyds digital services (reported). Lloyds and Halifax branch closures continue through October, with local-media angles. A Bank of England and CMORG sector-wide cloud-outage simulation was reported as completed on 9 October.

Brief as: reported, with the date of the report. These are mostly single-source; check the bank's own statement before quoting.

Bank tax: what is known and what is not

Known (with status)

  • CONFIRMED Budget date 28 October, Chancellor John Healey's first. gov.uk letter
  • REPORTED Banks currently pay the 3% corporation tax surcharge plus the bank levy. HMRC figures cited by BM Magazine: levy £1.3bn and surcharge £1.0bn in 2024-25.
  • REPORTED 6 Oct: Healey and City Minister Lucy Rigby met bank and building society chiefs; "listening mode", no decision. Attendee lists differ between outlets; Treasury sources would not confirm them. BM Magazine
  • REPORTED 9 Oct: UK Finance, CBI, TheCityUK, AFME and the City of London Corporation wrote to the Chancellor asking for a pledge of no further bank taxes, citing a UK bank tax burden of about 46.5%. This is Money
  • REPORTED 9 Oct: TUC estimates (advocacy, not official) of extra revenue over four years: raising the 3% surcharge to 16% about £24bn, to 35% about £60bn. Guardian
  • REPORTED Bloomberg (2 Oct): City fears a windfall tax on domestic banks with UK arms of overseas lenders exempt. Bloomberg

Not known

  • Whether any bank-specific measure will be announced at all.
  • The form if there is one: permanent surcharge rise, one-off windfall levy, change to the bank levy, or a change to reserves remuneration on Bank of England balances (floated in commentary).
  • Size, start date, and whether UK arms of overseas banks are exempt.
  • The Budget statement start time. The Commons Order Paper is the source; it was not retrieved.
  • Fiscal headroom. Figures differ by source: about £24bn inherited per one report, as low as £5bn per a Times report in September. Treat both as REPORTED.
Attribution rule. The 6 October meeting account rests on anonymous sourcing. Say "reported" and name the outlet. Lobbying estimates (the 46.5% figure, the TUC revenue numbers) belong to their authors, not to government.

10Briefing desk

For analysts building daily RI executive briefings for banking clients: reference numbers, wording rules, what to pre-build, and what to check each morning.

Reference numbers as at 10 October

Wording rules for the window

  • Mention is not event. A reported tax idea is not a Budget measure. Use "reported", "is understood to" and name the outlet.
  • Separate voices. Government, the Bank of England, trade bodies (UK Finance, CBI), unions (TUC) and think-tanks (IFS, Resolution Foundation) each get their own attribution.
  • Estimates belong to their author. TUC revenue figures, the 46.5% tax-burden claim and market-implied rate probabilities are advocacy or market estimates.
  • Motor finance: say "partly suspended", give both hearing windows, quote provisions as "provided to date", never a final cost. The FCA's own figure is about £7.5bn redress (12.1m agreements); press headlines of £9bn–£9.1bn appear to include about £1.6bn of administration costs.
  • Use the new names. Phoenix Group is now Standard Life plc; the Halifax House Price Index is now the Lloyds House Price Index (reported rename from July 2026); Hargreaves Lansdown and Direct Line are no longer listed.
  • Check the clock change. UK clocks go back on Sunday 25 October, so NatWest's 07:00 on 30 October is GMT (as its page states). Confirm time zones on the other results.

Pre-build checklist

  • By Fri 16 Oct: Budget lines for scenarios A to D; a savings and ISA note; a housing and mortgage note.
  • By Tue 20 Oct: data-run template for 20–21 October (labour market, CPI, public finances) with the August comparators in the table above.
  • By Wed 21 Oct: results templates for Barclays, HSBC, Santander UK, Lloyds and NatWest with guidance anchors and motor finance lines pre-filled.
  • By Fri 23 Oct: Budget-day run sheet with the Commons order paper time, and a same-day reaction list (IfG 15:00, IFS, trade bodies).
  • By Tue 3 Nov: MPC hold and hike versions, with the Budget's effect on the November Monetary Policy Report.
  • Agree who covers 20–21 October and 27–30 October; both are the heaviest stretches.

Morning checks (07:00, Monday to Friday)

  • FCA site and Upper Tribunal listings for a motor finance order or statement.
  • Commons business statement for a second-reading date on the Financial Services and Markets Bill, and the Order Paper for Budget-day timing once published.
  • Treasury Committee page for added sessions; the page showed only 13 and 14 October at the last read.
  • Lloyds and Nationwide newsrooms for house-price index release dates, which are unverified for October data.
  • Any bank's own statement before quoting a reported job-cut, closure or incident story.

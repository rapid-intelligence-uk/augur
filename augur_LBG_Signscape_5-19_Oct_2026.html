Pressure points

Three situations that need more than a date.

Motor finance redress

Regulatory · Watch · Black Horse is the Group's motor finance brand

The FCA's final rules are out. The live question is whether the scheme survives legal challenge, and when. Earlier guidance to "monitor the FCA site for the Policy Statement" no longer applies.

  • 30 MarFCA publishes PS26/3, the final rules: two schemes covering agreements from April 2007 to November 2024, with estimated redress of £7.5bn and a total bill of £9.1bn.
  • 1 and 8 MayFour legal challenges confirmed (CA Auto Finance, Consumer Voice, Mercedes-Benz and Volkswagen financial services).
  • JulyUpper Tribunal partially suspends the schemes. Lenders need not calculate or pay under the suspended parts but must prepare.
  • 19 AugFCA feedback on lenders' implementation plans flags gaps in population identification, calculators and quality assurance.
  • 1 SepCounty-court appeal judgment (Berry v Black Horse) reportedly rejects a limitation defence (claimant-side report).
  • 5–6 OctCase-management hearing on disclosure, expert evidence and timetable.
  • 14–18 Dec or 16–26 FebMain hearing, five days or nine days respectively.
  • Mid-2027Earliest redress payments on one claimant-side estimate, and only if the scheme is upheld.
Group position
  • Provision of £1.95bn at 30 June, with no extra charge in H1.
  • Reported to have chosen to join the scheme rather than challenge it.
  • FCA average redress estimate is about £829 per eligible agreement.
  • A claim of about £66m against Black Horse was reported in March.
Peers
  • Barclays holds £430m. Other captive lenders have provisioned heavily (BMW Financial Services £611.6m).
  • Barclays reports on 22 Oct and will give the first provision read-across before Lloyds on 29 Oct.
Watch for
  • Tribunal directions naming the main-hearing window.
  • Any FCA statement following the hearing.
  • Changes to peers' provisions on 22 and 27 Oct.
  • Movement in the Group's provision on 29 Oct (call LBG-F1).
Prepare
  • Update this brief on 6–7 Oct.
  • Hold a reactive line for a Tribunal outcome or an FCA statement.
  • Track claims-firm and law-firm activity against Black Horse.
Sources: PS26/3 summary · FCA implementation feedback · Hearing preview · Daily Record. Berry and £66m claim from claimant-side and press reports; verify.

Budget meets results, 27–30 October

Political and results · Clash · outside the client horizon, inside the planning horizon

The Budget is Wednesday 28 October, and the Group reports the next morning. Lloyds' own results will be read through whatever the Chancellor announces.

  • Tue 27HSBC Q3 results.
  • Wed 28Budget, expected around 12:30 after PMQs, with the OBR forecast on the same day.
  • Thu 29Lloyds Q3 statement, plus Bank of England Money and Credit.
  • Fri 30NatWest Q3 results; Lloyds Business Barometer likely around the last working day.
Context
  • John Healey became Chancellor on 20 July under Prime Minister Andy Burnham; this is his first Budget.
  • Reported commitments: no rises in income tax, employee National Insurance or VAT.
  • Borrowing was reported £8.1bn over forecast in the first five months, which narrows room for manoeuvre.
  • The £12,000 Cash ISA limit from April 2027 is already part of the baseline, with final regulations due this autumn (single source).
Group context
  • H1 net interest margin was 3.19%; 2026 guidance of underlying NII above £14.9bn was reiterated.
  • The "Accelerate 2030" strategy was launched with the half-year results on 30 July.
  • Full-year results are on 28 January 2027.
Watch for
  • Savings and ISA changes, property taxation, and any banking-specific measures (no source; speculative).
  • Overnight briefings on 26–27 Oct that pre-empt the statement.
Prepare
  • Reactive templates for Group economic, savings and mortgage commentary.
  • A single analyst rota covering 27–30 Oct, with a named lead for 28–29 Oct.
  • Draft cross-references so the Lloyds briefing can cite Budget measures the same morning.
Sources: Budget date summary · second source · Lloyds financial calendar. Budget date to be confirmed at gov.uk.

Data, rates and housing

Economic · Clash · 5 Oct to 5 Nov

Inflation is rising, the Bank of England is split, business confidence has dropped, and Halifax is showing falling house prices. The 20–21 October releases are the last major data before the Budget and the MPC.

Where things stand
  • CPI was 3.1% in August, the highest since December 2025.
  • Bank Rate is 3.75%. The 17 September hold was reportedly 6–3, with three members voting to raise it. One secondary source put a quarter-point rise on 5 November as the favourite; treat with caution.
  • Lloyds Business Barometer (Sept): confidence 41%, down 12 points, the lowest since April 2025, on energy prices and Middle East tensions. Economic optimism fell 18 points to 31%.
  • Halifax (Aug): prices down 0.2% on the month and 0.4% on the year, the first annual fall since November 2023.
What to do
  • Pre-draft consumer and rate commentary for 20 and 21 Oct.
  • Set Halifax September (c. 7 Oct) against the ONS house-price release on 21 Oct.
  • Prepare hold and rise scenarios for 5 Nov.
  • Track the October Barometer for a second weak reading.
Sources: ONS inflation · Bank of England · Business Barometer, Sept · Commons Library calendar. Halifax and MPC-vote details from press and secondary sites; verify.

About this edition

Method, limits and changes.

  • Method. Dates come from company and institutional calendars, official statistics schedules, and press reporting, combined with recurrence patterns from the augur Archive. Where a public date existed it replaced the pattern.
  • Limits. Nearly every source is secondary. Treat "Dated" as "reported", and use the register's tick box to record primary-source checks. No client intelligence was supplied for this cycle.
  • Changes from the earlier agent draft. The Budget is on 28 October, outside the window. ONS Labour Market and CPI are on 20 and 21 October, not 13–15 October. The FCA's motor finance rules are already published; the live item is the Tribunal. Lloyds, Barclays, HSBC and NatWest results dates are confirmed. Halifax and the Business Barometer timings are corrected. Conference season is a note, not a clash. The change of Prime Minister and Chancellor is added.
